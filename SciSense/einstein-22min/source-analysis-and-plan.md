## 2. Why the plan shouldn't use its dialogue

Building a 22-minute video out of this lecture's audio would be a re-cut of World Science Festival's copyrighted work:

1. **Content ID.** World Science Festival manages its catalog. A 22-min video that's mostly their audio will almost certainly get claimed (revenue goes to them) or **blocked**. If they file a takedown instead, that's a **copyright strike**. Three strikes means channel termination.
2. **Reused-content rejection.** YouTube's monetization review rejects channels whose videos are mainly someone else's content, even when stock footage is placed over it. Envato visuals don't change whose *voice and words* the video is built on.
3. **Fair use doesn't cover it.** Using most of a lecture's narration as the backbone of your own video, for the same educational purpose, is the opposite of the short, transformative use fair use protects.

**What you *can* freely use from it:** the **physics, the facts and the overall order of ideas.** Ideas aren't copyrightable; Greene's *wording* and his *original story devices* are. So:

| Avoid copying (his creative expression) | Use instead (free) |
|---|---|
| George & Gracie characters | Your own characters, or none |
| Forward-land / Backward-land treaty signing | **Einstein's own lightning-and-train thought experiment** from his 1916 book *Relativity*, which is public domain |
| "Evil George" jousting parable | Your own E = mc² explanation (e.g., the Sun losing 4 million tonnes of mass every second) |
| Alien on a bicycle sweeping to Beethoven | Your own "now slice" example |
| Car going north then east | Your own version (e.g., a plane flying north vs. north-east) |
| His sentences | An original script, read by your narrator |

**Bonus:** Einstein's own popular writing (*Relativity: The Special and General Theory*, 1916; English translation 1920) is **public domain**. Short Einstein quotes read by a second voice ("In his own words…") give the doc an authentic layer that's 100% safe.

For real Greene voice: use the **3 CC BY Greene clips** already in the time documentary (Columbia Alumni 2014). Reuse 1–2 of them here.
