## A3 — The Legal Crime: Origin Story   (<=700w)

ORIGIN STORY BEATS: (narrative order — claim — [tag] — [S##])

1. **The lawful practice, named.** US standard of care for a postpartum patient in psychiatric distress: outpatient-first, medication-led, inpatient stays measured in days, the baby not admitted with the mother, discharge into follow-up split across several prescribers. In this case the prosecution called that care adequate — the state's own position [A1 §12][D2]. — [VERIFIED as description] — [S25][S49][S52][S55]

2. **Two mid-1950s shocks emptied the asylums.** State psychiatric hospitals peaked at 558,922 residents in 1955 [S101]. Then chlorpromazine (Thorazine), FDA-cleared 1954, made discharge imaginable and gave overcrowded hospitals a cheap alternative to custody [S102]; a civil-rights-era revulsion at the asylum as inhumane supplied the moral push [S101]. Freeing people was a genuine rights victory. — [VERIFIED] — [S101][S102]

3. **The 1963 promise, half-kept.** Kennedy signs the Community Mental Health Act, Oct 1963: federal money for ~1,500 community centers to catch the discharged. About half were ever built; sustained operating funds never came [S100]. From 1970 to 2018 the US closed ~84% of state hospital beds [S103]. The hospitals emptied; the replacement did not get built. — [VERIFIED] — [S100][S103]

4. **The underfunding was written into law, not neglected.** Medicaid, 1965, carries the "Institution for Mental Diseases" exclusion: no federal payment for adults 21–64 in a psychiatric facility over 16 beds — designed to keep Washington out of asylum funding and pin inpatient care on the states. Public psychiatric beds are now down ~90–97% from peak [S105]. — [VERIFIED on statute] — [S105]

5. **The retreat was ratified.** Carter's Mental Health Systems Act (1980) tried to renew federal commitment; Reagan's Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act (1981) repealed most of it within a year and block-granted the rest. Federal mental-health spending fell; nobody rebuilt the bed base [S104]. — [1-SOURCE] — [S104]

6. **Managed care set the length of the stay.** Through the 1980s–90s, employers and Medicaid handed psychiatric benefits to "behavioral health carve-out" firms whose core tool is utilization review — preadmission, continued-stay, concurrent authorization. Massachusetts' own Medicaid carve-out cut length of stay for severe mental illness from 22.5 to 17.8 days in year one [S106]. The short stay is a purchasing decision, not a clinical discovery. — [1-SOURCE] — [S106]

7. **Britain built the mother-baby unit; the US didn't.** Joint admission of mother and infant was pioneered by Thomas Main at the Cassel Hospital in 1948 and is now a commissioned NHS network of ~22 units [S108][S52]. America's first perinatal psychiatric inpatient unit opened at UNC in June 2011 — 63 years later — and still doesn't admit the baby [S49][S50]. The blockers are the ones already in place: the IMD rule, liability, and that an infant can't be licensed as a patient [S55]. — [VERIFIED / 1-SOURCE on counts] — [S108][S52][S49][S50][S55]

8. **The diagnosis was never written down.** DSM-IV (1994) added a "postpartum onset" specifier — a footnote on major depression or bipolar disorder, onset within 4 weeks only. DSM-5 (2013) widened it to "peripartum onset" and kept it a specifier [S107]. Postpartum psychosis has no code of its own in the DSM or the ICD [S109]. — [VERIFIED] — [S107][S109]

9. **What the omission does downstream.** No category means no criteria to teach, so training skips it — "we don't train our doctors in it… it's massively missed" [S30]. It means no clean box for screening tools, insurance coding, or trial enrollment; treatment guidance rests on naturalistic cohorts and expert consensus, not controlled trials; dedicated research funding is harder to win. Since 2020 an international consortium has petitioned the APA to make it a distinct disorder [S109]. — [VERIFIED] — [S30][S109]

10. **The unsettling sum.** Each decision was lawful and defensible on its own day — freeing patients, real medicine, curbing overbilling, cautious nosology. The 2023 postpartum mother inherited all of them at once: no bed, no dyad unit, a stay sized by a reviewer, an illness with no name, follow-up split among prescribers never trained to see it. No one built this to cause harm. — [frame] — [A2 §CASE RECORD][S25]

ACTORS & MOTIVES: (institution/force — what it wanted — what it produced)
- **Congress + Kennedy (1963)** — humane community care, cost savings, a legacy — emptied hospitals and half a network [S100].
- **Congress (Medicaid, 1965)** — keep the federal government out of asylum funding; states to own inpatient care — a permanent financial brake on psychiatric bed capacity (IMD exclusion) [S105].
- **Reagan administration + Congress (1981)** — cut federal spending, devolve to states — block grants and a frozen bed base [S104].
- **Pharmaceutical industry + academic psychiatry (1954 on)** — effective treatment, a scientific psychiatry — the psychopharmacology turn that made "medication-led, outpatient" the default and outpatient polypharmacy routine [S102].
- **Employers + Medicaid agencies + carve-out firms (1980s–90s)** — control runaway inpatient costs — utilization review and the days-not-weeks stay [S106].
- **Hospitals / throughput economics** — turn beds over, limit liability — visitation-only units, no dyad admission [S55].
- **APA / DSM task forces (1994, 2013)** — cautious, evidence-bounded classification — a specifier instead of a diagnosis, and the training, coding, screening and research gaps that follow [S107][S109].
- **No one** — wanted three dead children.

GOOD-FAITH vs. HARM LEDGER:
| Good-faith reform | What it cost-shifted / broke |
|---|---|
| Ending custodial asylums was a civil-rights win; conditions were real and cruel [S101] | Bed closures were executed; the funded community replacement was ~half-built and never sustained [S100][S103] |
| Thorazine and the drugs after it were real medicine; many people did go home [S102] | "Medication-led, outpatient" hardened into the only well-funded option; polypharmacy across many prescribers became normal [S102][A2 §CASE RECORD] |
| The IMD exclusion (1965) meant to stop warehousing and force better models [S105] | It also permanently defunded inpatient psychiatric capacity, including specialized perinatal beds |
| Utilization review answered genuine overbilling and needless long stays [S106] | Length of stay became a line-item a reviewer sets, not a recovery a clinician judges |
| The DSM specifier was added by clinicians trying to name something real [S107] | A specifier isn't taught, coded, screened for, or funded as a disease is [S30][S109] |
| Block-granting (1981) was sold as state flexibility [S104] | Federal mental-health funding fell and was never restored |

TIMELINE SEED:
- **1948** — Thomas Main opens joint mother-infant psychiatric admission, Cassel Hospital, England [S108]
- **1954** — FDA clears chlorpromazine (Thorazine); clinical psychopharmacology begins [S102]
- **1955** — US state psychiatric hospital census peaks at 558,922 [S101]
- **Oct 1963** — Community Mental Health Act signed; ~1,500 centers planned, ~half built [S100]
- **1965** — Medicaid enacted with the IMD exclusion (16-bed rule, ages 21–64) [S105]
- **1970–2018** — US closes ~84% of state hospital beds [S103]
- **1980** — Mental Health Systems Act (Carter) [S104]
- **1981** — Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act repeals it; block grants (Reagan) [S104]
- **1980s–1990s** — behavioral-health carve-outs and utilization review spread; psychiatric length of stay falls to days [S106]
- **1994** — DSM-IV adds "postpartum onset" specifier (4-week window) [S107]
- **June 2011** — first US perinatal psychiatric inpatient unit opens, UNC; baby not admitted [S49][S50]
- **2013** — DSM-5 changes it to "with peripartum onset"; still a specifier [S107]
- **2020** — international consortium begins petitioning the APA to classify postpartum psychosis as a distinct disorder [S109]
- **2023** — ~5 US inpatient perinatal units; 45 states + DC with no maternal-mental-health inpatient/residential program [S49][S54]
