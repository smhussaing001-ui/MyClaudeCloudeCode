## A6 — Early Resistance   (~500 words)

WARNINGS:
- **James Alexander Hamilton**, California psychiatrist — founded the International Marcé Society, Manchester, July 1980; co-edited *Postpartum Psychiatric Illness: A Picture Puzzle* (1992). Argued for decades that postpartum psychiatric illness was distinct, recognizable, treatable — and ignored by US medicine — [S143][S142]
- **Nancy Berchtold** — 1985, New Jersey — after her own postpartum psychosis, founded Depression After Delivery, the first US postpartum peer/advocacy network, "fueled by anger at a medical hierarchy that refused to place much importance" on the illness — [S142]
- **Jane Honikman** — 28 June 1987, Santa Barbara — founded Postpartum Support International to build the lay support the health system did not — [S140][S141]
- **DSM refusal.** Advocates (Hamilton, the Marcé Society, later PSI) repeatedly asked the APA to make postpartum psychosis a distinct diagnosis. DSM-IV (1994): only a "postpartum onset" specifier capped at 4 weeks; rationale — it "does not have enough unique attributes to justify a separate listing." DSM-5 (2013): refused again, renamed it "peripartum onset"; committee held the evidence "did not demonstrate" it is distinct. Still not a diagnosis in 2026 — [S155] `[VERIFIED on outcome]`
- **Margaret Spinelli**, Columbia psychiatrist — *Am J Psychiatry*, Sept 2004, "Maternal Infanticide Associated With Mental Illness: Prevention and the Promise of Saved Lives" — post-Yates, called for DSM clarification, mandatory clinician education, screening, treatment guidelines — [S154]

EARLY FIXES ATTEMPTED:
- **Texas "Andrea Yates Law"** (2003, expanded 2005) — after Yates drowned her five children, June 2001 — narrowed to requiring providers hand new mothers *information*; no screening, no funding. Outcome: ~575,000 brochures by 2014; mandatory screening never enacted — [S152]
- **New Jersey S-213** ("Mother's Law") — signed April 2006, effective Oct 2006 — first US state to mandate postpartum depression screening; backed by Gov. Richard Codey and Mary Jo Codey. Outcome: peer-reviewed evaluation (*Health Affairs* 2011) found **no change** in treatment initiation for Medicaid mothers — under 7% before and after — because it carried no funding, no enforcement, no reimbursement — [S157][S156]
- First US perinatal psychiatric inpatient unit did not open until June 2011 (UNC), a decade after Yates — [A2 §INFRASTRUCTURE][S50]

LEGISLATIVE TRAIL:
- **Melanie Blocker Stokes Act.** Stokes died by suicide 7 June 2001 in postpartum psychosis; ~30 days later Rep. Bobby Rush (D-IL) introduced the Postpartum Depression Research and Care Act. Reintroduced 2001, 2003, 2005, 2007, 2009.
  - 110th Congress: House passed 15 Oct 2007, **382–3** (Roll 963). Died in the Senate, no vote.
  - 111th Congress: reintroduced as the "MOTHERS Act" (Rush + Sen. Menendez). House passed 30 Mar 2009, **391–8**. Stalled a year in Senate HELP amid an organized Scientology-linked campaign (Citizens Commission on Human Rights) calling it coerced drugging of mothers.
  - Enacted only by being folded into the ACA as **Section 2952**, signed 23 Mar 2010 — nine years on.
  - **STRIPPED:** the standalone service-grant program became "encourage" research, an awareness campaign, voluntary grants, and merely "a study on the benefits of screening." $3M/yr authorized; **Congress never appropriated any**. HHS omitted §2952 from its 2012 list of the ACA's benefits for women — [S144][S145][S146][S147][S148][S149][S150][S151]
- **Texas HB 3318** (2009, Rep. Jessica Farrar) — would have created a distinct lesser "infanticide" offense (max 2 yrs) for a mother who kills a child under 1 while judgment is disturbed by childbirth/lactation; drafted with George Parnham. Passed House Criminal Jurisprudence Committee, sent to Calendars, **never got a floor vote**. Refiled later; never enacted. Illinois (2018) remains the only US enactment, and only as a sentencing mitigator — [A2 §LAW][S153]

WHY THEY STALLED:
- DSM: APA held the evidence did not establish postpartum psychosis as clinically distinct — [S155]
- MOTHERS Act: repeated Senate inaction; organized Scientology-linked opposition; survived only as an **unfunded** ACA rider — authorized, never appropriated — [S148][S150][S151]
- NJ / Texas: passed only after being narrowed to education, with no money, no enforcement, no screening requirement with teeth — [S152][S156]

FOR SCRIPT:
1. "A mother founded America's first postpartum support network in 1985 — out of anger that her doctors wouldn't take the illness seriously. Forty years later, it still isn't a diagnosis." [S142][S155]
2. "The bill was named for a Chicago mother who died in 2001. The House passed it, 382 to 3. It took nine years and a rider on another law to become anything — and Congress never funded it." [S144][S145][S149][S151]
3. "New Jersey made screening mandatory in 2006. Researchers checked five years later. For mothers on Medicaid, nothing had changed — the law came with no money and no teeth." [S156]
