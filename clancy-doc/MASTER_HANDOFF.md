**COLD OPEN — A2's pick [S36]:** 14 state review committees, 2008–17. When a pregnancy-related death was
caused by a mental health condition, they judged it preventable in **100% of cases** — vs 64% for all
other causes. Peer-reviewed, CDC-authored, says nothing about this defendant, cannot prejudice the trial.

**ACT 2 — origin [A3]:** Medicaid's **1965 IMD exclusion** barred federal payment for adults in
psychiatric facilities >16 beds — the single most damning lawful decision; it structurally guaranteed the
missing beds. Plus: postpartum psychosis left a DSM *specifier*, never a diagnosis (1994, unchanged 2013)
— nothing to teach, code, screen for, or fund research on [S107][S109].
Line: *"Nobody built this system to hurt anyone. It was assembled one reasonable law at a time — a
hospital emptied here, a budget cut there, a hospital stay shortened by a claims reviewer — until a
mother in crisis had almost nowhere to land."*

**ACT 2 — loophole [A4]:** ACOG/USPSTF *recommend* screening; the ACA makes insurers *cover* it; **no law
requires anyone to perform it** [S57]. FDA regulates drug marketing, not medical practice. Pregnant
patients structurally excluded from trials. 2024 parity rule left unenforced from 15 May 2025 [S61][S62].
Line: *"You are not measured against the best care. You are measured against the usual care. When the
usual care has a gap in it, following it is still a defense. The gap is legal."*

**ACT 3 — impact segments [A5], in order:** treatment cascade → culture/silence → five units →
economics → mortality → equity → **ACT-OUT: ~two dozen countries have infanticide statutes recognising
postpartum illness; the US has none. Illinois 2018 is a sentencing mitigator only** [S31][S72][S73][S74].
Best translation: *"Line up a hundred women who got depressed after having a baby. Thirty are noticed by
anyone. Sixteen get some treatment. Six get enough of it. Three get well. The other ninety-seven carry
it, most of them looking fine."* [S44]

**ACT 4 — they were warned [A6]:** Melanie Blocker Stokes died by suicide in postpartum psychosis
7 Jun 2001. Rep. Bobby Rush introduced a bill in her name ~30 days later and every Congress to 2009. The
House passed it **382–3** (15 Oct 2007) and **391–8** (30 Mar 2009); the Senate let it die both times. It
became law only folded into the ACA §2952 (23 Mar 2010), stripped to "encourage research," an awareness
campaign, and *a study on the benefits of screening*. Congress never appropriated a dollar; HHS omitted
it from its own 2012 list of the ACA's benefits for women [S144–S151].
Also: DSM category sought and refused 1994 and 2013 [S155]; NJ's 2006 first-in-nation screening mandate
changed nothing for Medicaid mothers, unfunded and unenforced [S156]; Texas post-Yates infanticide bill
cleared committee 2009, never got a floor vote [S153].

**ACT 5 — measures [A7]:** WORKED — Medicaid 12-month postpartum extension, 48–49 states + DC, closing
the window when ~63% of these deaths occur [S170][S36]; MCPAP for Moms is the only model with an RCT
(EPDS −4.2) [S169]. DIDN'T — hotline reports 80,305 contacts and 91% answered <30s, and **zero published
data on whether any caller reached care** [S162]; 2016 Shadows Act funded $5M/yr, 7 states, never
nationally evaluated [S160].
**THE ACCESS PARADOX (strongest single beat):** brexanolone, first drug ever FDA-approved for postpartum
depression — $34,000, 60-hour inpatient infusion, REMS — uptake so low the maker discontinued it in 2024
and **the FDA withdrew it in 2025. It no longer exists on the US market.** Oral successor zuranolone
($15,900/course) reached ~**1%** of PPD patients in year one [S164][S165][S166].
**Beds: net change since this case = zero.** Still 5 units, 45 states with none, no dyadic unit anywhere
[S167]. And contracting: HRSA hotline staff laid off 2025; FY26 budget proposes eliminating HRSA and
cutting $274M from Maternal & Child Health [S163].
