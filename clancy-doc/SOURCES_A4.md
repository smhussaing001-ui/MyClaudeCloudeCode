## OFF-LABEL PRESCRIBING / FDA AUTHORITY

[S120] "Off-Label Use vs Off-Label Marketing of Drugs. Part 1: Off-Label Use — Patient Harms and Prescriber Responsibilities" — JACC: Basic to Translational Science (Elsevier/American College of Cardiology), 2023, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2452302X23000116 — supports: the FDA regulates the marketing, labeling and safety of drugs but "does not regulate the practice of medicine"; physicians are legally free to prescribe an approved drug for uses, doses, populations and combinations not on the label. ⚠️ Search-summary only; peer-reviewed journal, non-controversial legal point widely corroborated. `[1-SOURCE]`

[S121] "Off-Label Use of Prescription Drugs" — Congressional Research Service, Report R45792, updated 23 Feb 2021, https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R45792 (PDF: https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R45792/R45792.3.pdf) — supports: physicians "are legally free to use [approved drugs] for purposes other than that for which they were approved," off-label use can be accepted medical practice; the FDA does not regulate physician prescribing, only manufacturer promotion. ❌ HTTP 403 from this environment on both URLs; content via search indexing of the CRS report. **A9 should open the PDF directly.** `[1-SOURCE]`

## RESEARCH EXCLUSION OF PREGNANT / LACTATING PEOPLE

[S122] "Advancing Clinical Research With Pregnant and Lactating Populations" / "Inclusion of Pregnant and Lactating Persons in Clinical Trials" — National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, consensus study, 2024, NCBI Bookshelf NBK605204 (Legal Liability) and NBK589758 (The Legal Landscape), https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK605204/ — supports: pregnant and lactating people are routinely excluded from drug trials; fear of legal liability is "pervasive" while actual liability is not — no liability claim identified arising from a pregnant or lactating trial participant since 1962; IRBs are often hesitant and may lack obstetric/pharmacology expertise; "liability" is frequently conflated with reputational risk, cost and trial complexity. ⚠️ Search-summary only; National Academies consensus report — high reliability. `[1-SOURCE]` pending direct read.

[S123] "Excluding pregnant and nursing people from clinical trials causes more harm than it prevents, study finds" — Brown University School of Public Health (news release on a peer-reviewed modeling study), 29 May 2025, https://sph.brown.edu/news/2025-05-29/testing-during-pregnancy — supports: the Thalidomide crisis arose because pregnant people were NOT studied before the drug was used in pregnancy; modeling shows a completed pregnancy trial would have prevented ~99.6% of Thalidomide birth defects; blanket exclusion increases net harm and post-market liability rather than reducing it. ⚠️ Search-summary only; university release summarizing a published study. `[1-SOURCE]`

## STANDARD OF CARE AS LEGAL SHIELD

[S124] "Legal Defenses to Medical Malpractice" — FindLaw, reviewed 2024, https://www.findlaw.com/injury/medical-malpractice/defenses-to-medical-malpractice-patients-negligence.html ; "Common Defenses in Medical Malpractice Cases" — LawInfo, https://www.lawinfo.com/resources/medical-malpractice/common-defenses-in-medical-malpractice-cases.html — supports: malpractice liability requires proof that the provider failed to meet the standard of care (what a reasonably competent peer would do); asserting the standard was met is a core defense; adherence to clinical guidelines is a recognized defense; the "respectable minority"/"two schools of thought" doctrine protects a clinician who followed one of several accepted approaches. ⚠️ Legal-explainer sites; the doctrine itself is black-letter law. `[1-SOURCE]`

[S125] "Modernizing the Medical Malpractice Standard of Care" — Southwestern Law Review, Philip G. Peters Jr., 2024, https://www.swlaw.edu/sites/default/files/2024-05/Article%209_Peters.pdf — supports: in most US jurisdictions the legal standard of care is set by customary professional practice, so conformity to custom is ordinarily a complete defense and litigation does not readily push the standard upward. ⚠️ Not fetched; law-review article, author is a recognized authority on the malpractice standard of care. `[1-SOURCE]`

## MENTAL HEALTH PARITY — NON-ENFORCEMENT

[S127] "US District Court Grants Freeze on Mental Health Parity Enforcement" — Morgan Lewis (ML BeneBits), 2025, https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/mlbenebits/2025/05/us-district-court-grants-freeze-on-mental-health-parity-enforcement ; "Departments Pause Enforcement of MHPAEA Final Rule…" — Groom Law Group, 2025, https://www.groom.com/resources/departments-pause-enforcement-of-mhpaea-final-rule-and-reconsider-mhpaea-enforcement-program/ ; "Regulators Pause Mental Health Parity Rules Enforcement" — Ogletree Deakins, 2025, https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/regulators-pause-mental-health-parity-rules-enforcement/ — supports: the ERISA Industry Committee (ERIC) sued the Departments on 17 Jan 2025 over the 2024 Final Rule; on 12 May 2025 the US District Court for DC stayed the case at the Departments' request; on 15 May 2025 Labor/HHS/Treasury announced non-enforcement of the 2024 rule's new provisions — and of pre-existing non-compliance — until the litigation is finally resolved plus 18 months; plans directed to rely on the 2013 regulations; Departments considering rescinding/modifying the rule. ⚠️ Three independent law-firm advisories; corroborates shared-bank [S61]. `[1-SOURCE]`

[S128] "What is mental health parity?" — healthinsurance.org glossary, https://www.healthinsurance.org/glossary/mental-health-parity/ ; "Insurance coverage and parity for mental health and substance use disorder services" — Texas Department of Insurance, https://www.tdi.texas.gov/health/mental-health-parity-overview.html — supports: MHPAEA does not independently mandate any mental-health coverage — it requires parity only where MH/SUD benefits are already offered; self-funded employer (ERISA) plans are exempt from state mental-health mandates; DOL enforcement tools are limited — it cannot assess civil monetary penalties for parity violations and relies on voluntary correction. ⚠️ Consumer-explainer + state regulator page; the statutory structure is well established. `[1-SOURCE]`
