## A8 — What More Can Be Done   (~450w)

SYSTEMIC ASKS: (ask — who proposes it — [S##] — strongest objection)
1. **Mandate screening, don't just recommend it.** MMHLA and state legislators (NJ 2006 was first; NY enacted one in 2026) — [S197][A2 §INFRA]. A mandate would require a validated tool (e.g. EPDS) plus documented referral at defined OB/pediatric visits. **Objection (serious):** screening without treatment capacity can harm — ACOG itself says screening alone doesn't improve outcomes, and NJ's unfunded 2006 law pushed positive-screen women into a system with nowhere to send them [S198][S197]. Answer: tie any mandate to funded referral pathways and a follow-up measure — screen-and-refer, not screen-and-abandon.
2. **Build dyadic mother–baby units.** RCPsych/NHS model; MGH and PSI call for US dyadic capacity [S52][S55]. UK cost: ~£707/bed-day vs £385 generic ward; ~£60,007 per admission-to-1-month vs £13,673 [S203]. **No one has publicly costed a US version** [A7]. **Objection:** the UK's own ESMI evaluation found a 0% probability MBUs are cost-effective per QALY; any payoff is long-term, via fewer readmissions [S203]. Ask framed as safety infrastructure, not a QALY buy.
3. **Repeal or waive the Medicaid IMD exclusion** [A3 §4] — NABH and behavioral-health hospitals; ≥4 full-repeal bills in the 117th–118th Congress; CBO has costed the options [S199][S200]. The structural ask: it is why perinatal psych beds don't get built. **Objection:** Mental Health America opposes repeal, arguing the bed shortage has other causes and repeal drains community care [S201]. Narrower path: the SUPPORT Act (2018) already carved a pregnancy/SUD exception — extend it to perinatal mental health [S202].
4. **Enforce parity.** Revive the 2024 MHPAEA rule suspended 15 May 2025 [A4 §5][S61]; APA and AHA want enforcement resumed. The Mental Health and MAMA Act (H.R.7227/S.3698, 2026) goes further — no cost-sharing for MH/SUD care from pregnancy through 12 months postpartum [S209]. **Objection:** employer plans (ERIC) call the rule unworkable and are litigating it.
5. **A distinct DSM/ICD category for postpartum psychosis** — 2025 international expert-consensus panel; Spinelli's 2020 APA petition [S204][A3 §8-9]. Unlocks teachable criteria, billing codes, trial enrollment, dedicated research funding — current guidance rests on naturalistic cohorts. **Objection:** APA nosologists favor the cautious "specifier"; a stand-alone code risks over-medicalizing new motherhood.
6. **Legal recognition of postpartum illness** (~2 dozen countries) [A2 §LAW][S31]. **FOR:** the gender-neutral *McHoul* standard fits these cases poorly; Illinois (2018) shows a mitigation path [S74]. **AGAINST (take seriously):** equal-protection / sex-discrimination problem; feminist critique that a women-only statute revives the "hormonal, less-culpable woman" stereotype and infantilizes; Hatters-Friedman — men get depressed too, and an NGRI defense already exists [S206][S207]. Present as genuinely contested, not obvious.

AUDIENCE ACTIONS:
- **What to say to a new mother:** ask directly — "How are you actually sleeping? Any thoughts that scare you?" — then listen, and offer one concrete thing (take the baby two hours; drive her to an appointment). **Don't say** "enjoy every moment," "you should be grateful," "it'll pass," or "just try sleep/exercise." If she describes psychosis-type symptoms, don't leave her alone with the baby — treat it as ER-level [S195][S196].
- **Look up your state:** policycentermmh.org — 2026 Maternal Mental Health State Report Cards, interactive map (US graded "F" on new parental-support measures; no state earned an A) [S208][S54].
- **Support / donate:** Postpartum Support International (postpartum.net); the Marcé Society / North American branch (marcesociety.com) — the research body behind the DSM push.
- **Civic action:** check whether your US House member and senators cosponsor the **Moms Matter Act** (S.4552 / H.R.8811) or the **Mental Health and MAMA Act** (H.R.7227 / S.3698); Capitol switchboard **202-224-3121**, ask them to cosponsor. Both sit in committee as of Aug 2026 [S209].

WARNING SIGNS: (PSI + Policy Center for Maternal Mental Health — postpartum psychosis is a medical emergency; onset usually the first 2 weeks, up to a year — [S195][S196])
Rapid onset · not sleeping but not tired (decreased *need* for sleep) · confusion or disorganized thinking · delusions or strange beliefs · hallucinations · paranoia/suspiciousness · rapid mood swings · agitation or unusual energy · intrusive thoughts of harm. Any of these: call 988 or the hotline now, and do not leave mother and baby alone.
