## A4 — The Loophole

WHY LEGAL — MECHANISM (the chain, step by step):
1. **Screening.** ACOG, ACNM and the USPSTF all recommend universal perinatal depression and anxiety screening. Under the ACA, most private plans must cover USPSTF Grade A/B services with no cost-sharing. But no federal law requires any clinician to perform the screen, and no penalty exists for skipping it [S57][A2 §INFRA]. Coverage without a requirement — that is the loophole in one sentence.
2. **Prescribing.** The FDA regulates a drug's marketing and labeling, not the practice of medicine [S120][S121]. Once a drug is approved for any use, a licensed physician may lawfully prescribe it for any condition, at any dose, in any combination, with no trial data for that use [S121]. In pregnancy and lactation almost everything is off-label, because those patients are routinely excluded from the trials [S122][S123].
3. **Why the evidence vacuum is structural, not accidental.** Fear of liability is "pervasive," yet actual liability is near-zero — no claim identified from a pregnant trial participant since 1962 [S122]; IRBs without obstetric expertise decline such studies [S122]; the Thalidomide legacy hard-wired the instinct to keep pregnant bodies out of research, though Thalidomide harm came from *not* testing in pregnancy [S122][S123]. Result: the least-studied population in pharmacology, treated with untested combinations — lawfully.
4. **Length of stay.** In general, inpatient psychiatric stays are re-authorized every few days by an insurer's concurrent utilization review — usually a nurse applying proprietary "medical necessity" criteria (InterQual, MCG); when criteria are no longer met, coverage for further days ends [S126]. Per DECISIONS this is stated only as the general mechanism, not asserted about any individual discharge.
5. **Parity, unenforced.** The Mental Health Parity Act never mandates mental-health coverage — only parity where coverage exists [S128]. The 2024 rule that tightened it [S62] was sued by the ERISA Industry Committee (17 Jan 2025); on 15 May 2025 Labor, HHS and Treasury announced they will not enforce it, or pursue prior violations, until the case resolves plus 18 months [S61][S127]. Plans revert to the weaker 2013 rules; DOL cannot levy civil penalties for parity breaches regardless [S128]. For a patient denied continued inpatient care: the appeal right survives, but the federal enforcers have publicly stepped back.

WHY NEVER PREVENTED:
- No federal screening mandate has ever been enacted; the maternal-mental-health laws that passed (Into the Light; TRIUMPH for New Moms, 2022) fund grants, a hotline and a task force — none require screening or treatment [S81][S57].
- Medicine is regulated by the states, not the FDA; no body can require combination-drug evidence before use [S120][S121].
- Research exclusion of pregnant and lactating people persists despite federal recommendations to end it [S122][S123].
- Standard-of-care doctrine rewards conformity — no clinician is negligent for doing what peers do — so litigation never forces the standard upward [S124][S125].
- Parity enforcement is suspended [S61][S127]; even before, DOL had no civil-penalty power [S128].

STANDARD-OF-CARE SHIELD (script-ready):
"In American medicine you are not judged against the best care. You are judged against the usual care. Do what your colleagues would have done and the law protects you — even when the usual care was not enough. When the standard itself has a hole in it, following it is still a complete defense. The hole is the rule."

BENEFICIARIES:
- Insurers and self-funded employer plans (the ERISA Industry Committee): shorter authorized stays, fewer parity audits, lower spend [S127][S128].
- Drug manufacturers: off-label use widens a drug's real market with no duty to test it in pregnancy or in combination [S120][S121].
- Clinicians and hospitals: conformity is the cheapest malpractice protection there is [S124][S125].
- The cost lands elsewhere — a modelled $14.0bn for one year's untreated perinatal mood and anxiety disorders, most of it carried by mothers and families [S59].
- Tried and failed: ACOG has urged universal screening since 2015 — still no mandate [S57]; the 2024 parity rule, the strongest attempt to force insurer compliance, was frozen within months [S61][S62][S127]; Illinois's 2018 law, the only US statute to name postpartum illness, merely mitigates a sentence [S74][A2 §LAW].

FOR SCRIPT:
1. "There is a recommendation to screen every new mother. There is insurance coverage for it. There is no law that says anyone has to do it. Coverage without a requirement — that is the whole trick."
2. "A doctor can prescribe any approved drug, in any combination, to a pregnant patient, with nothing published behind it — because the FDA regulates how a drug is sold, not how medicine is practiced. And pregnant patients are kept out of the studies that would tell us more."
3. "You are not measured against the best care. You are measured against the usual care. When the usual care has a gap in it, following it is still a defense. The gap is legal."
