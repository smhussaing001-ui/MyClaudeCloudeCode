[S140] "About Us" / "Featured PSI Member: Jane Honikman" — Postpartum Support International, accessed 28 Aug 2026, https://postpartum.net/about-psi/ · https://postpartum.net/featured-psi-member-jane-honikman/ — PSI founded 28 June 1987, Santa Barbara CA, by Jane Honikman at the first conference on "Women's Mental Health Following Childbirth"; predecessor lay groups: Postpartum Education for Parents (Honikman, 1970s), Depression After Delivery (NJ), Pacific Post Partum Support Society (Vancouver), PaNDA (Melbourne). ⚠️ org's own history page.

[S141] "History and Purpose of Postpartum Support International" — Journal of Obstetric, Gynecologic & Neonatal Nursing (JOGNN), 2017, https://www.jognn.org/article/S0884-2175(17)30435-5/fulltext — PSI mission and founding; awareness of perinatal emotional change among public and professionals. ⚠️ not fetched in full; peer-reviewed nursing journal.

[S142] "Blue: A History of Postpartum Depression in America" (Rachel Louise Moran, Univ. of Chicago Press) via "A History of Postpartum Depression in the United States" — Unsung History podcast/transcript, https://www.unsunghistorypodcast.com/postpartum-depression/ · https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/B/bo235431923.html — Nancy Berchtold had postpartum psychosis/depression after a 1983 birth; in 1985 founded Depression After Delivery (DAD), the first specifically postpartum support/self-help/advocacy group in the US, "fueled by anger at a medical hierarchy that refused to place much importance on postpartum depression and psychosis"; DAD chapters spread nationally late 1980s–1990s. James Hamilton mentored the lay movement. ⚠️ secondary (historian's account via podcast); book is scholarly.

[S143] "History" — International Marcé Society for Perinatal Mental Health, https://marcesociety.com/about/history/ · "History of the Marcé Society (1980–2016)" PDF, https://marcesociety.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/11/Marce-Society-History-1980-2016_nine_1September2016.pdf · Wikipedia "International Marcé Society" — Society launched at the first academic meeting on Puerperal Mental Disorders, Manchester, July 1980; James Alexander Hamilton a founder; named for Louis Victor Marcé (1858 treatise, first work devoted to puerperal mental illness); forum to bring psychiatrists, endocrinologists, social scientists together on postpartum illness. ⚠️ org history + Wikipedia.

[S144] "Melanie's Story: How One Woman's Tragic Encounter With Post Partum Psychosis Led to Landmark Women's Health Legislation" — HuffPost, Susan Dowd Stone, 2010 (updated), https://www.huffpost.com/entry/melanies-story-how-one-wo_b_564925 — Melanie Blocker Stokes, 40, Spelman grad, Chicago, married to physician Sam Stokes; gave birth to daughter Sommer Skyy in 2001; postpartum psychosis ~1 week after delivery; ECT and hospitalization; died by suicide (fall from a Chicago hotel roof) 7 June 2001. Mother Carol Blocker became a national advocate. Rep. Bobby Rush (D-IL) introduced the Melanie Blocker Stokes Postpartum Depression, Research, and Care Act in 2001; reintroduced each Congress. ✅ — advocacy-authored personal essay; bio details corroborated across coverage.

[S145] "H.R. 20 (110th Congress): Melanie Blocker-Stokes Postpartum Depression Research and Care Act" — Congress.gov / GovTrack / H.Rept. 110-375, https://www.congress.gov/bill/110th-congress/house-bill/20/all-actions · https://www.govtrack.us/congress/bills/110/hr20 · https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-110hrpt375/html/CRPT-110hrpt375.htm — subcommittee hearing 1 May 2007; House passed 15 Oct 2007 by suspension, 382 yeas / 3 nays (Roll no. 963), as amended; never passed by the Senate. ✅ (via congress.gov all-actions text) — primary legislative record.

[S146] "House Report 111-48 — Melanie Blocker Stokes MOTHERS Act" — U.S. House Committee on Energy and Commerce, 2009, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-111hrpt48/html/CRPT-111hrpt48.htm — HR 20 (111th Cong.) provisions: (1) encourages HHS to continue/expand research on postpartum depression and psychosis (basic, epidemiologic, diagnostic, clinical, awareness); (2) authorizes grants to public/nonprofit entities for services (outpatient, inpatient management, education, support). CBO estimate: <$500,000 in 2009, ~$9 million over 2009–2014; $3 million authorized for FY2009. No dissenting views; approved by voice vote; CBO found no federalism cost (state participation "voluntary"). Notes a similar bill was heard in the 110th Congress (hearing 1 May 2007). ✅ — primary committee report.

[S148] "MOTHERS Act Legislation Faces Opposition" — MGH Center for Women's Mental Health, https://womensmentalhealth.org/posts/mothers-act-legislation-faces-opposition/ · "NJ's The Record Covers Scientology's Opposition to the MOTHERS Act" — Postpartum Progress (Katherine Stone), https://postpartumprogress.com/njs-the-record · "MOTHERS Act To Drug America's Moms For Fake Postpartum Depression" — Postpartum Progress, https://postpartumprogress.com/postpartum-depression-mothers-act-dont-let-the-women-children-families-suffer — organized opposition from the Church of Scientology–affiliated Citizens Commission on Human Rights and allied campaigners framing the bill as coerced screening/drugging of mothers; the bill was held up in the Senate HELP committee (per the North Jersey Record). ⚠️ headline/snippet + advocacy blog; the Scientology/CCHR link and Senate HELP holdup are widely reported.

[S149] "MOTHERS Act Becomes Law" — MGH Center for Women's Mental Health, https://womensmentalhealth.org/posts/mothers-act-becomes-law/ · "Melanie Blocker Stokes MOTHERS Act Becomes Law With Passage of Healthcare Reform" — Postpartum Progress, https://postpartumprogress.com/melanie-blocker-stokes-mothers-act-becomes-law-with-passage-of-healthcare-reform-postpartum-depression · ACA §2952, PL 111-148, 23 Mar 2010, https://www.congress.gov/111/plaws/publ148/PLAW-111publ148.pdf — the MOTHERS Act was enacted only as Section 2952 of the ACA ("Support, Education, and Research for Postpartum Depression"), signed 23 March 2010: HHS to coordinate research, run a national public-awareness campaign, and "conduct a study on the benefits of screening" for postpartum depression/psychosis; ~$3M authorized. ⚠️ secondary summaries + primary public law text (not fetched in full).

[S152] "Andrea Yates' Legacy: Mandatory Postpartum Screening?" — The Texas Tribune, 6 Aug 2014, https://www.texastribune.org/2014/08/06/andrea-yates-legacy-mandatory-postpartum-screening/ — after Andrea Yates drowned her five children (June 2001, Houston), the 2003 "Andrea Yates Law" (sponsored via defense attorney George Parnham and wife Mary) required hospitals/providers to give patients information about postpartum illness; revised/expanded 2005. Parnhams' Yates Children Memorial Fund + Mental Health America of Greater Houston trained 2,500+ professionals and distributed 575,000+ brochures by 2014. Mandatory screening not enacted; Dr. Lucy Puryear: "the next move is to universally screen … so it's not some sort of hit or miss." ✅ — established outlet.

[S153] "Texas Could Be First State to Have Infanticide Law" — NBC 5 Dallas–Fort Worth, 2009, https://www.nbcdfw.com/news/local/texas-could-be-first-state-to-have-infanticide-law/2117548/ · Texas HB 3318, 81st Legislature (2009), Rep. Jessica Farrar, bill analysis (Committee Report / Substituted), https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/81R/analysis/html/HB03318H.htm · "Infanticide Bill Passes House Committee" — Texas Values, https://txvalues.org/infanticide-bill-passes-house-committee-im-not-kidding-it-really-did/ — HB 3318 would have let a jury that found a mother guilty of murdering her child under 12 months, whose judgment was impaired by childbirth/lactation, instead convict of "infanticide," a state-jail felony (180 days–2 years); drafted with George Parnham. Passed the House Criminal Jurisprudence Committee (April 2009), referred to Calendars Committee, but never received a House floor vote; died at session's end. Farrar refiled the concept in later sessions; never enacted. ⚠️ news + primary bill analysis (bill status via Texas Legislature Online, not fetched directly here — DNS failure); committee passage and Calendars referral confirmed in coverage.

[S154] "Maternal Infanticide Associated With Mental Illness: Prevention and the Promise of Saved Lives" — American Journal of Psychiatry, Margaret G. Spinelli, MD (Columbia / NY State Psychiatric Institute), Sept 2004; 161(9):1548–1557, https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.161.9.1548 (PubMed 15337641) — post-Yates review arguing a high proportion of maternal infanticide occurs in the context of postpartum mental illness and calling on the psychiatric community to improve prevention: DSM-IV criteria clarification, mandatory medical/professional education, screening, and treatment guidelines. ⚠️ abstract/citation via authoritative indexing; full text behind login. Peer-reviewed. Spinelli also edited *Infanticide: Psychosocial and Legal Perspectives on Mothers Who Kill* (APPI, 2003).

[S155] "Postpartum Psychosis: Experts Seek a Distinct Category Within the DSM" — Postpartum Support International, https://postpartum.net/postpartum-psychosis-experts-seek-a-distinct-category-within-the-dsm/ · "Postpartum psychosis: a diagnosis for the DSMV" — Archives of Women's Mental Health, 2021 (PubMed 34494144), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34494144/ · "International Consortium … Support Classification of Postpartum Psychosis as a Distinct Disease" — Mount Sinai newsroom, 2025, https://www.mountsinai.org/about/newsroom/2025/... · Wikipedia "Postpartum psychosis" — DSM-IV (1994) reintroduced postpartum illness only as a "with postpartum onset" specifier limited to 4 weeks; prevailing view that postpartum psychosis "does not have enough unique attributes to justify a separate listing in the DSM-IV." DSM-5 (2013) changed the specifier to "with peripartum onset," again declining a distinct category; committee position that submissions "did not demonstrate" postpartum psychosis is a distinct diagnosis. 2021 and 2025 expert consortia (incl. Veerle Bergink) renewed the push; still not adopted as of 2026. ⚠️ mix of advocacy page, peer-reviewed abstract, institutional release, Wikipedia; the "not enough unique attributes" phrasing is from secondary/tertiary sources, not the DSM sourcebook.

[S156] "New Jersey's Efforts To Improve Postpartum Depression Care Did Not Change Treatment Patterns for Women on Medicaid" — Health Affairs, Kozhimannil KB, Adams AS, Soumerai SB, Busch AB, Huskamp HA, Feb 2011; 30(2):293–301, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3736568/ — evaluated NJ's 2005 education initiative and Oct-2006 mandatory-screening law against 30,955 continuously enrolled Medicaid deliveries (July 2004–Oct 2007). Fewer than 7% of women initiated mental-health care within six months of delivery in every period; the law produced no significant change in treatment initiation, follow-up, or continued care. Authors attribute the null result to no financial incentive/screening payment, no enforcement mechanism, low Medicaid reimbursement, lack of service integration, and unaddressed patient-level barriers. ✅ — peer-reviewed. (Some secondary sources misfile this as Health Services Research; it is Health Affairs.)

[S157] "N.J. starts screening for postpartum depression" — NBC News / AP, 2006, https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna12500035 · "Codey Bill To Require Postpartum Depression Screening And Education" — NJ Senate Democrats, https://www.njsendems.org/... · NJ State Law Library legislative-history checklist, P.L. 2006 c.12 (N.J.S.A. 26:2-176), https://repo.njstatelib.org/... — New Jersey was the first US state to mandate postpartum depression screening: bill S-213 (Sen. Richard Codey and Mary Jo Codey, who had postpartum depression after a 1984 birth. ⚠️ news + party press release + library checklist; "first state" widely reported.
