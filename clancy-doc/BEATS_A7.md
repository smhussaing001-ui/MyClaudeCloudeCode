## A7 — Long-Term Measures   (~590 words)

MEASURES:
- 2016-12-13 — **Bringing Postpartum Depression Out of the Shadows Act**, enacted inside the 21st Century Cures Act; authorized $5M/yr (FY18–22) in state grants for maternal-depression screening, provider training and referral — [S160]
- 2022-12 — **TRIUMPH for New Moms Act** (created the federal Task Force → *National Strategy to Improve Maternal Mental Health Care*, published 2024-05-14) and **Into the Light for MMH Act** (reauthorized the state grants + the Hotline, FY23–28; FY23 appropriation $7M Hotline / $10M grants) — [S161][S172]
- 2022-05-08 — **National Maternal Mental Health Hotline** launched, 1-833-TLC-MAMA / 1-833-852-6262 — [S162]
- 2019-03 brexanolone (Zulresso) · 2023-08 zuranolone (Zurzuvae) — first drugs **FDA-approved specifically for postpartum depression** — [S164][S166]
- 2014 — **MCPAP for Moms** (Massachusetts); model now replicated in ~30 state/regional access programs, up from 15 in 2021 — [S168]
- 2022-04 → 2025 — **Medicaid 12-month postpartum coverage**: 48–49 states + DC (only Arkansas, Wisconsin outstanding, both moving in 2025). Prior cliff: coverage ended at 60 days, though ~63% of maternal mental-health deaths fall 43–365 days postpartum — [S170][S36]

EFFECTIVENESS:
- **Hotline:** 80,305 contacts since 2022; FY25 33,720 (+35% YoY); 91% answered <30s; >12,000 referrals; acuity 97% low / <1% high. Post-call satisfaction surveyed and high. **No data published on whether callers reached care or recovered** — "effectiveness" is reported as call volume and answer speed [S162].
- **Shadows Act:** 30 states/territories applied in 2018; only 7 funded (FL KS LA MT NC RI VT), each built an access program. **Funded, never evaluated as a national cohort** [S160].
- **Access programs:** MCPAP for Moms enrolled >80% of MA obstetric practices, 11,500+ patients. Its one RCT (PRISM, *Lancet Public Health* 2024, 312 patients): EPDS fell 4.2 points; adding intensive practice-level support added nothing (4.3). Clinically meaningful, **not remission** — roughly a third stayed symptomatic [S168][S169].
- **Screening:** providers asking rose to ~88% of postpartum visits (PRAMS 2020–21) from ~79% (2018), but the HEDIS screening+follow-up measure is still **voluntary**, and the 2016 treatment cascade (30.8% identified → 6.3% adequately treated → 3.2% remission) has no newer national replacement. Recommendation-without-mandate moved the asking, not the treating [S171][S44].
- **Beds:** still **5** inpatient perinatal psychiatric units (AR CA LA NY NC); 45 states have none; none admit the infant overnight. Woman's Hospital, Baton Rouge opened a 10-bed unit Sept 2024 — a swap inside the five, not growth. **Net change since [S49]: zero** [S167].

THE ACCESS PARADOX:
- **Brexanolone (Zulresso):** ~$34,000/course, 60-hour inpatient IV infusion, REMS. Uptake so low that Sage discontinued it Oct 2024; FDA withdrew approval effective 2025-04-14. The first drug ever approved for PPD **no longer exists on the US market** [S166].
- **Zuranolone (Zurzuvae):** $15,900 for the 14-day course; ~$0 with a commercial copay card, but Medicaid/uninsured patients hit prior-auth (AL and NC Medicaid require failing two antidepressants first). ~6,600 prescriptions in year one against ~500,000 PPD cases/yr — roughly 1% [S164][S165].
- The Hotline's front door works; the rooms behind it — 5 beds, 45 states with no residential program, too few specialists in 48 states [S54] — are unchanged.
- **MCPAP for Moms runs in Massachusetts, and this case still happened there.** Per DECISIONS B6: evidence about the **ceiling** of consultation-only reform (a psychiatrist advising an OB is not a bed, a diagnosis, or continuity), not proof the model fails [S56][S169].

GAPS REMAINING (worst first — feeds A8):
1. **Inpatient capacity:** no net gain since the case; no mother-baby (dyadic) unit anywhere in the US; 45 states with zero residential option [S167].
2. **Federal infrastructure is now contracting:** HRSA hotline staff laid off in 2025 (some reinstated); FY26 budget proposes eliminating HRSA and cutting $274M from Maternal & Child Health. Every measure above runs through that agency [S163]. `[VERDICT-SENSITIVE: political framing — keep factual]`
3. **No mandate anywhere:** screening, follow-up and the HEDIS measure remain voluntary; the 2016 cascade is still the last word on downstream treatment [S171][S44].
4. **Drugs exist but are economically gated,** and the inpatient-only one is gone [S164][S166].
5. **National Strategy (2024) is unfunded and non-binding** — a plan with no appropriation [S172].
6. **Postpartum Medicaid** near-universal, but coverage ≠ providers; 2 states still cut off at 60 days [S170].
